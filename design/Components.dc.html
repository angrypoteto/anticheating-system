Proctorly — the navy system

One palette, one type ramp and one set of components, so a screen a student meets under exam pressure and a screen an administrator meets on a Tuesday afternoon are recognisably the same product. Every screen on this canvas is built from what follows.

Colour

Navy carries the chrome and every primary action; the surfaces are cool paper rather than pure white so the navy has something to sit on. One accent only — teal, for subjects and links — because a second competing accent is how a palette stops meaning anything. Green, amber and red are reserved for status and never used decoratively, so a colour a teacher sees always says the same thing.

Type

Source Serif 4 carries titles only — it gives an exam system the institutional weight a grotesque cannot — and Geist does all the work below that. Numbers are tabular everywhere they can be compared: scores in a column that jitter as they update are a column nobody trusts.

Buttons

One primary action per screen, sized by how often it is pressed and how much it costs to miss — Fitts’s law: Start exam is 52px tall, a row action is 34px. Destructive actions never look like the primary; they are quiet until confirmed, then unmistakably red (the isolation effect — the one dangerous thing must not look like its neighbours).

Fields

Focus is a navy border plus a soft ring — visible without shouting, and never colour alone. An error keeps the message beside the field rather than at the top of the form, where nobody reads it.

Status

Similarity: one form for one meaning. A pill always reports a state the system is in; it is never a button, and nothing that is a button ever wears one. Every status pill carries a word as well as a colour, so it survives a colour-blind reader and a grayscale print-out.

Figures

Common region: a bordered card is the enclosure that says “these four numbers belong together”. The number leads, the label sits above it small, and the qualifier below stops a figure being read as more than it is — 78% of those who submitted, not of the class.

Telling somebody something

Three registers, and the wording matters more than the colour. A warning during an exam says what will happen next; a refusal says what to do instead; a confirmation names what it is about to destroy, in numbers, before anyone agrees to it.

Warning 1 of 3 — you left the exam window

Leaving is recorded each time, however you leave. Two more and your exam is submitted as it stands.

This account wrote exams

Delete those first, or disable the account instead — disabling keeps their results and stops them signing in.

Sitting reopened

Answers kept, 3 warnings cleared, and they can answer until 4:35 PM even though the exam is closed.

Navigation

Nine flat links became four named groups — chunking, so the eye picks a group before it picks a link. The active item is a filled navy block rather than a tinted word: at a glance from across a desk, position and fill read faster than colour.
